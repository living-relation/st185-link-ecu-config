Step order for a new Link setup

The grouping follows the course's own Setup → Tuning → Advanced layout. The exact order inside it is my synthesis, not something Evans states publicly. Source: Link course outline, pre-tuning checklist.

  1. Car ready: wiring, grounds, charging 13.5–14 V, fuel pressure 40–50 psi on a gauge, boost leak test, wastegate spring and 3-port solenoid checked.
  2. Software + calibration file: install, load base file.
  3. Basic setup: sensor inputs, outputs, injector data, cam and crank trigger.
  4. Start and run: cranking, post-start and warm-up fuel; idle.
  5. Main fuel table (VE), with datalogs to check.
  6. Closed-loop lambda once the open-loop map is close.
  7. Spark timing, then knock control.
  8. Transients: acceleration enrichment, individual cylinder trims.
  9. Boost control: open loop, then closed loop.
  10. Extras: DBW tuning, flex fuel, launch and traction control, engine protection.
  11. Re-log and recheck. Any hardware change means a retune.

Pre-tuning checklist free

  • Electrical first: no exposed harness sections, crimped or soldered joints, clean bare-metal grounds, battery ground at least as thick as the power cable.
  • Fuel: base pressure 40–50 psi on a real gauge (first suspect for any AFR problem), clean filtered supply, upgrade the regulator with big pumps.
  • Engine: compression within about 20 psi across cylinders, bleed the cooling system, timing belt or chain installed correctly.
  • Turbo: leak test, route wastegate and BOV lines right, use a 3-port solenoid, check spring size.
  • Spark plugs: at least one heat range colder, gap about 0.025–0.030" on boost.
For Dan's car: the Walbro 450 counts as a high-flow pump, so confirm the regulator suits it and rail pressure rises with boost. The page says nothing on wideband, base timing, trigger or injectors.

Injector data & sizing free

  • Injectors have a small delay (dead time) between firing and flowing. The ECU needs accurate data for it, mostly at idle and light load where pulses are short.
  • At very short pulses an injector stops flowing in proportion. Big injectors hit this at idle, which gives a lean, unstable idle or misfires.
  • Don't richen the mixture to hide that. It can hurt the engine over time.
  • E85 needs roughly 30–40% more flow than gasoline, and its longer pulses help keep a big injector in its linear range.
  • Prefer injectors with published, trustworthy data (Injector Dynamics is the author's pick).
For Dan's car: 1400cc EV14s on a 2.19 L engine are big for idle on gas. Flex fuel helps. Enter proper dead-time data before judging idle.

Boost control paid course

  • The Link course has open-loop and closed-loop boost control modules.
  • Free: when the ECU controls boost, use a 3-port solenoid. 4-port ones make control so sensitive that closed-loop is nearly impossible.
  • Free: an oversized wastegate spring leaves you unable to turn boost down and makes control touchy. Check it before tuning.
  • Free: a boost-creep problem in cold weather can hit the boost cut. Electronic control copes better than a manual controller.
For Dan's car: the EFR 7163 almost certainly uses an internal wastegate. The solenoid and spring advice still applies, but I found no Evans page about EFR specifically.

Turbo selection free

  • Compressor flow (lb/min) roughly times 10 gives peak wheel horsepower.
  • Leave 10–15% flow headroom if you sit at peak power. It keeps shaft speed and intake heat down.
  • Match turbine to engine size. A too-large turbine makes lag, and too small hurts compressor efficiency.
  • Bigger flow means more lag. The author favors a usable power band over peak numbers.
For Dan's car: background only. The turbo is already chosen.

Datalogging free

  • Logs show what happened and let you compare runs.
  • Worth logging: fuel pressure (should rise with boost), oil pressure and temperature, air-fuel ratio, exhaust temperature, knock, exhaust back pressure.
  • Check channel count, logging speed, and log capacity of the ECU.
  • The Link course has datalogging basics and log analysis modules.
For Dan's car: make fuel pressure and rail-vs-boost a logged channel from day one.

Remote tuning workflow free

  • Send build details, get a quote (usually $300–600, more for flex fuel).
  • Get a close base calibration, then log real driving and send it back.
  • Several revise-and-log rounds follow.

After the tune free

  • Any hardware change means a retune.
  • If something shifts: check fuel pressure, battery voltage, grounds, timing, loose connectors, and a failing wideband sensor.
  • Oil and plug intervals are tighter for turbo street cars.
