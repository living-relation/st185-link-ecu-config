A/C amp research: 1993 ST185 auto climate control

Updated 2026-10-04: ACT correction added, see ECU ↔ A/C amp wires. Earlier follow-up: plan chosen = keep the auto amp, Link replaces A15. See Follow-up and How the A/C will work.
For Dan's 1993 USDM ST185 (5S-GTE swap, Link G4X XtremeX, COP ignition, magnetic-clutch-only compressor). Read-only research, 2026-10-04. Every claim has a page tag like EWD p.211. Source list is at the bottom. "Not in docs" means I couldn't find it, so I didn't guess.

Recommendation (short version)

Your three problems aren't the auto amp's problems. The RPM signal, the compressor sensor, and the coolant input all go to the regular A/C amplifier (A15). The auto amp (A31/A32) doesn't need any of them EWD p.210–211 Mitchell A15 test table.

So I'd flip your plan around: keep the auto amp (all of its sensors are in the cabin and already wired from the factory) and retire the regular A/C amp (A15). Then let the Link G4X run the compressor. That means the A/C request, the evaporator anti-freeze cycling, the clutch relay, and the RPM, throttle and coolant cuts all move to the Link. Wire the dual pressure switch in series with the clutch relay coil so it still protects the system in hardware.

How the A/C will work (new design)

Step by step

  1. Blower on. The heater relay powers the blower, and the same supply powers the 10A A/C fuse EWD p.211. The auto amp (AUTO) or the panel dial (LO/M/HI) sets the speed. Nothing changes here.
  2. Press A/C. The panel switch puts 12 V on V-G. The Link sees it on An Volt 10. The light needs its ground, see step 8.
  3. Pressure check (hardware). The A/C fuse feeds A5 pin 1. If pressure is between 30 and 384 psi, A5 passes 12 V out pin 4 (Y-R) to the k_acc coil +. Outside that range the coil has no power, so the clutch can't engage no matter what the Link does EWD p.218.
  4. Link decides. If the A/C button is on, the evaporator is above the "on" point (~4 °C to start), throttle and RPM are under your cut limits, and ECT is OK, the Link raises idle (ETB A/C step), waits a short delay, then grounds the k_acc coil −.
  5. Clutch engages. k_acc 30→87 powers A4 pin 4. The Link runs the condenser fan (k_fan2) whenever the clutch is on. The radiator fan logic is unchanged.
  6. Cycling (anti-freeze). When A24 element 1–2 drops to the "off" point (~1 °C to start), the Link drops the clutch. When it warms back to ~4 °C, it re-engages. This is the job A15 pin 16 used to do.
  7. Cuts. Pressure fault → A5 opens (hardware). Wide-open throttle, high RPM or hot coolant → Link drops the clutch (software). There's no compressor-lock check anymore, because your compressor has no sensor.
  8. A/C light. Ground the panel indicator return (R-B, old A15 pin 1). The light shows whenever the button is on. Idle-up VSV A3: not used (ETB does idle).

Correction (2026-10-04): US ECU ↔ A/C amp wires, cross-checked with forums

Verdict: yes, the A/C cut wire exists on a US 1993 ST185. It's called ACT, at engine ECU E9 (connector A) pin 21. The wire is LG-B at the ECU, changes to G-Y after IJ1-3, and lands on A/C amp A15 pin 11. Signal flow is ECU → amp: the ECU tells the amp to drop the compressor. Dan is right.

My earlier "US ECU has AC only, no ACT/ATS" claim was wrong. It came from one document: manuals-electrical\3SGTE ECU Pinouts.pdf. That's a third-party, Japanese-language summary sheet, and its row "ST185後期 (USA&CND) 89661-2B290" leaves the ACT and ATS cells blank. Two Toyota documents, a 1990 US EWD, Link's own ST185 plug-in pinout, and US-harness forum posts all show ACT on the ST185. The sheet's US-MR2 rows also lack ACT, and US MR2s really don't have it. My guess is the ST185 row was copied from the MR2 row, but that's not proven.

Design impact: none. A15 is removed and the Link drives the clutch relay directly (the approach in the SW20 thread's option c). ACT, ATS and the AC sense all go away. The Link applies its own TPS / RPM / ECT cuts in its A/C clutch function, and it knows the clutch state because it drives the relay.

Two repo errors to fix (not edited — read-only):
  1. sot/channels.csv row "ACAMP,MG" and the fl_acamp_mg spur lead call A15 pin 5 / A4 pin 3 the magnetic clutch. That's wrong for 1993. The clutch coil is A4 pin 4 (B-W), fed by the clutch relay, and measures 3.4–3.8 Ω EWD p.210 Mitchell auto A/C "Clutch test". Pin 5 is a separate ~12 Ω coil that only exists on variable-displacement compressors Mitchell A15 table, note 1. HARNESS-RECONCILE-PLAN §9.4 calls pin 5 the "lock sensor". That's also off. The RPM (lock) sensor is on A15 pins 9/14 (W-L / W-R), 100–130 Ω Mitchell "RPM sensor test".
  2. channels.csv row "ACAMP,TW" calls TW an "A/C coolant switch on Engine-A". In 1993, TW is the A25 A/C water temp sensor (a thermistor) EWD p.211, mounted on the heater core housing in the dash Mitchell component locations. It needs no engine port and no new wire. Mostly right, but not the whole story. The auto amp also sets the temperature blend door (air-mix, through A23) and can pick the vent mode in AUTO EWD p.212–214, p.217 §5. One gotcha: even the manual LO/M/HI blower speeds need the auto amp in the circuit, because the heater relay ground path runs HR → BSW through it EWD p.216 §2.

1A/C button → Link (net name suggestion AC_BTN)Tap the V-G wire: panel A/C switch output, A17 (connector A) pin 14 → IJ1 pin 7 → old A15 pin 3 EWD p.211/213.
