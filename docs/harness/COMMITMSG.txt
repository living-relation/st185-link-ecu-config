plan 6.41-6.43: fuel pump circuit, segmented shielding, PDU power split

Fuel pump. Looked the pump up rather than guessing: Walbro F90000295, 450 LPH,
Radium bench test 14.4 A at 13.5 V. Design at 20 A for E85 and a 14.4 V charging
system. 12 AWG feed and ground, 25-30 A fuse, its own relay, local chassis ground
in the trunk. The 60 A figure is the right order for INRUSH, not steady draw -
wire and fuse size on continuous current, and the contact only has to survive a
millisecond spike. Industry hardwire kits for this pump ship a 30 A fuse and 40 A
relay, which is the practical answer to the transient.

The pump keeps its own connector, 12 inches from the pump, and that is the only
break the circuit needs. It is out of the inline connector entirely. If a service
disconnect is ever wanted, the part is a Deutsch size 12 contact (25 A, 20 of each
gender on hand), not the size 16 used elsewhere.

Fuel level sender uses the OEM connector, both halves on hand. Nothing to source.

The inline DT connector now carries seven signal conductors instead of nine, so
five ways spare. Packaging recorded: rear VRC at the rear driver-side frame in the
back seat area, tank all the way back in the trunk, pump and sender wires bundled
with the VRC harness but their connectors nowhere near each other.

6.42, shielding. A continuous screen is not practical and is not being built. Two
segments, each grounded at one end: sensor drop screens terminate on the VRC case
and float at the sensor; VRC output screens terminate at the ECU and float at the
box. Front and rear the same. The ABS sensor drops take their own routes after the
box, away from adjacent harnesses - raw VR is millivolts. Forward of the box the
signals are square waves, which is why the pump feed can share that bundle.

6.43, power. The PDU (the PMU-16, not the cabin PDB) owns most internal low-power
and accessory circuits directly. Every high consumer gets its own relay. Battery
kill goes to the spare HCR150 - 130 A, IP67, one of two owned, the other being the
EPS relay - under PDU control.

Three things flagged before that gets designed in: an HCR150 does not carry
cranking current, so it cannot break the starter feed; the PDU cannot sit
downstream of its own kill relay or it can never re-close it; and killing the
battery on a running alternator is a load dump, so excitation has to drop too.

Also recorded: the TE BOM lists no solid-state relays. All four relay types on it
are electromechanical. If solid-state parts exist from another order, their part
numbers need adding before anything is designed around them.
