plan 6.46: is the PDU worth buying at all - open decision, counted honestly

Daniel pushed back right after 6.44 landed: if both junction blocks stay and we
only inject a few feeds, why buy an expensive PDU when relays do it? He is right
to ask, and the numbers do not favour the PDU.

Counted what is actually kept. J/B No.1 keeps 10 fuses and 4 relays, R/B No.2 one
and one, R/B No.3 one relay, R/B No.4 three fuses and the heater relay - 14 fuses
and 7 relays, factory-wired, zero labour. J/B No.2 is the only block deleted, and
it held five circuits.

So under option B the PDU is a sixteen-channel device replacing five circuits and
doing some timing. PMU-16 is $1,499 today. The relay equivalent is 2-3 more micro
ISO relays on top of the 8 already owned, a second fuse block because the TE
2141029-1 is full at F1-F13, and about $150-250.

The part that makes it work is the CSB3. Its four low-side outputs are unassigned,
the ECU commands them on 0x643, and the ECU is alive through key-off on its own
hold power - so Pi hold, kill relay coil and alternator excite drop all have a free
home. They land exactly full at four, with the oil lamp as the fourth and nothing
spare, so that contention is written down.

Also checked option C while there: re-homing 14 fuses and 7 relays does not fit one
PMU-16 either, so full J/B deletion is a two-unit decision.

Recommendation recorded: drop it unless the per-channel current monitoring is
something he actively wants. Marked OPEN and flagged that 6.38, 6.44 and 6.45 all
need rewriting if it goes. Nothing further should be built on a PMU assumption
until this closes.
