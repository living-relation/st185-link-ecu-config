plan 6.44-6.45: PDU feeds buses not circuits, and the battery kill is settled

Option B, with the granularity defined properly - 6.44.

The constraint nobody had written down: inside a junction block the fuses share
internal buses, so a single fuse input cannot be reached without cutting into the
block. "Move a few circuits to the PDU" is therefore not free-form. The only
places a PDU output can land without dash surgery are the injection points the
J/B already has, and for J/B No.1 there are exactly three - always-hot at 1I-1,
AM1 at IE1-10, AM2 at IE1-17.

So the PDU takes AM1 and AM2. Both are ignition-switched, which makes the PDU the
ignition master and gives load-shed during crank and a clean dash drop on kill.
Always-hot stays a plain fused feed - it has to stay hot regardless, so there is
no control to gain, and it carries DEFOGGER's 30 A. Everything inside J/B No.1
and both kick-panel R/Bs is untouched. Two wires, no dash rewiring.

No new harness file either: loom C already carries oem_ie1, oem_jb1_1i, oem_rb2
and oem_rb4, so the injection loom Daniel expected to need already exists.

Channel budget 12 of 16, 4 spare. Flagged for measurement: the 40 A and 30 A on
AM1 and AM2 are fusible-link ratings, not loads, and a PMU output is 25 A. Added a
clamp-meter step to the pre-teardown probes in the redistribution execution order,
since it decides one channel or two paralleled per bus.

Battery kill - 6.45, closing the three questions from 6.43.

A four-cylinder petrol starter draws 100-300 A cranking and the 3S-GTE's
gear-reduction unit is in the upper half of that, against 130 A for the HCR150.
It is not in the starter path and never will be. Wrote the distinction down
explicitly because conflating the two is dangerous: the mechanical cutoff in the
trunk is the real master switch and covers the starter cable; the HCR150 is a
PDU-controlled main-bus disconnect for everything downstream of the PDB.

PDU feed position relative to the kill relay - disregarded at Daniel's direction.

Alternator excitation now drops with the kill, as a PDU sequenced shutdown rather
than a circuit: drop excitation, short delay for field collapse, then open the
relay. One channel, no extra hardware, delay measured on the bench. The mechanical
cutoff gets no such courtesy, which is what the alternator's load-dump rating is
for.
