93 pump

8.5:1 forged 5S-GTE on 93. MatchBot IAT at 25–30 psi is still in the 47 C region after a 4.5 in core — survivable, but 93 will want timing pulled as boost climbs. Street seed stays 18 psi; 22 psi is the planning ceiling until a knock-logged pull says otherwise.

Planning ceiling: 22 psi.

E85 + water/meth ≥27 psi

E85 at λ≈0.78 is not knock-limited at these cylinder pressures on 8.5:1. The ceiling is compressor choke / turbine dP / IAT, not octane. 30 psi is the hardware max, not a required point.

Engage ≥27 psi to cut compressor-out temp 20–40 C in the hottest island. That is extra IAT margin and can hold 28–30 psi farther into the redline band instead of tapering to 27. It is not free power — it is permission to keep the boost you already computed.

mdot = VE · (CID · RPM / 2 / 1728) · ρ · PR crank = mdot · (10 hp/lb-min on 93, 11 on E85) wheel = crank · (1 − 0.17)

04Bar-plate vs tube-and-fin

Same 27×12 in face, same 55 lb/min charge, same 60 mph stack airflow. The difference is the core, not the size. Effectiveness is calibrated into MatchBot's official "typical IC" high-speed band (85–90%). Thermal mass and recovery come from aluminum volume × Cp / UA.

Road car: most time is part-throttle. Tube-and-fin has ~half the metal mass of bar-and-plate at the same 4.5 in / 27x12 in face, so recovery at speed is faster and heat-soak after a pull-then-traffic is shorter. Steady-state ε is a few points lower; we buy that back with 4.5 in thickness (allowed). Bar-plate wins a 95% WOT duty cycle (time-attack, drag) where the extra UA stays useful and soak never has time to matter. Charge dP is also lower on tube-fin, which is compressor efficiency the turbo does not have to pay.

Idle / traffic soak (4.5 in)

Bar-plate 8.81 kg vs tube-fin 4.72 kg. After a pull the thicker metal store keeps heating the next charge slug until ram air returns. Recovery τ is 4.6 s tube-fin vs 5.4 s bar-plate at 60 mph — the road-car number that matters, because you are not at WOT 95% of the lap.

What 4–5 in buys

3 in tube-fin drops to ~61% ε on this face at 55 lb/min — too thin. 4.0–4.5 in is where tube-fin enters the 85% band. 5 in still helps bar-plate more than tube-fin (diminishing NTU). 4.5 in is the pick: fits the crash-bar depth the user allowed, without paying bar-plate soak.

05Front stack

Order, front to back: bumper / crash bar, FMIC (4.5 in tube-fin, 27 x 12 in face), oil cooler (low, off the rad face if possible), trans cooler (same), half-width AC condenser, Mishimoto racing radiator + slim fans.

Half-width condenser

60 mph radiator air: half-width 2.893 kg/s (1.11× need) vs full-width 2.696 kg/s (1.04×). The half-width unit sits passenger-side so the driver-side rad face sees ram air. A full condenser in front of a racing rad is how these cars overheat with the AC on.

Radiator + fans

Mishimoto 2-row racing (Celica / 5S-class), slim PWM pair behind. Two-row aluminum, Celica / 5S-class. Slim PWM pair behind the core, not a thick OE clutch fan. Oil and trans coolers live off the rad face (low in the opening or ducted) so they do not eat the remaining air.
