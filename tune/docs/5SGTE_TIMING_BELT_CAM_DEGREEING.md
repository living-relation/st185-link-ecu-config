NOTE Self-check the card before you cut a single degree. The 1 mm numbers and the centerline must agree, and
they do:
Intake: centerline ATDC = (duration ÷ 2) − opening BTDC = (227 ÷ 2) − 4 = 113.5 − 4 = 109.5° ≈ 110° ATDC ✓
Exhaust: centerline BTDC = (duration ÷ 2) − closing ATDC = (226 ÷ 2) − 10 = 113 − 10 = 103° BTDC ✓
Because the card is internally consistent, you can degree these cams two independent ways and they must
agree. If they do not, your true-TDC zero is wrong.
DANGER Do not use "105° / 117°." Those centerlines belong to the high-lift shim-under-bucket Gen 3 HKS 264 (10.3
mm / 9.8 mm lift). Your cams are the outer-shim RT063/RT064 set. Using the Gen 3 numbers on these cams
will mis-time the engine.

PHASE 1 TEARDOWN TO THE BELT
CAUTION Disconnect the negative battery terminal and wait at least 20 seconds before starting work (FSM caution,
EM-19).
1.1 — Strip access
RH front wheel → RH engine under cover → alternator → intercooler → EGR vacuum modulator and VSV → EGR valve and
pipe → throttle body → No.2 timing belt cover. On the ST185 the intercooler must come out before the RH engine mount
through-bolt will clear.
1.2 — Mark everything before you disturb it
Rotate to No.1 TDC compression and paint-mark the belt against all three sprockets plus the belt's direction of rotation. You
are about to lose the factory reference — make your own first.
1.3 — Break the crank bolt loose while it is still assembled
Use the crank holding SST or a flywheel lock. Do not use the starter. Never rotate the crankshaft counterclockwise.
1.4 — Remove
Tensioner (2 bolts) → belt → cam sprockets as required → crank pulley → No.1 cover → crank timing sprocket.
Fig 3 — Removing the two tensioner bolts (EM8006). Fig 4 — Lifting the belt off the cam pulleys (EM8007).
PHASE 2 FIND TRUE TDC — DO THIS BEFORE TRUSTING ANY MARK
DANGER This is the single most important step in the document. Every centerline you measure later is referenced
to this zero. A 3° error here becomes a 3° error in both cams, and on an interference build that is how valves
get bent.
2.1 — Mount the wheel
Fit the degree wheel to the crank snout with spacer washers so the retaining bolt does not pull it into the block. Fix a stiff
pointer to a block bolt. Remove all four spark plugs and rag the holes.
2.2 — Piston stop method
Bring No.1 near TDC, then rotate roughly 15° backward. Install the 14 mm screw-in stop. Rotate forward (clockwise) until
the piston touches the stop — record the reading. Rotate backward to the opposite contact — record. True TDC is exactly
halfway between the two readings. Set the wheel to zero there and lock it.
2.3 — Verify twice, then compare to the cast mark
Repeat the stop test from a different starting position. Only when two runs agree, compare your true zero against the crank
pulley groove and the "0" on the No.1 timing belt cover. A 3–5° disagreement is common and normal. Trust the degree
wheel. Do not adjust anything to make the cast mark agree.
2.4 — Note the ATI damper mark
The ATI Super Damper carries laser-etched 360° timing marks. Treat them as a reference to confirm against your true zero,
not as a source of truth. Scribe your verified TDC onto the damper.

PHASE 3 INSTALL THE BELT ON FACTORY MARKS
Set the belt exactly per Toyota first. Degreeing in Phase 4/5 is an adjustment from a known-good baseline — it is not a
substitute for getting the belt on correctly.
CAUTION The engine must be COLD when the belt is installed and tensioned (FSM notice, EM-28). The hydraulic
tensioner will not set correctly on a warm engine.
3.1 — Oil pump pulley
Align the cutouts of pulley and shaft, slide on, install the nut with SST 09616-30011. Torque 355 kg-cm (26 ft-lb, 35 N·m).
3.2 — Crankshaft timing pulley
Align the set key with the key groove. Slide on with the flange side facing inward.
3.3 — No.2 idler pulley
Torque 440 kg-cm (32 ft-lb, 43 N·m). Confirm it spins smoothly.
3.4 — No.1 idler pulley
Apply Three Bond 1344 / Loctite 242 to two or three threads of the pivot bolt. Install the plate washer and pulley. Torque 530
kg-cm (38 ft-lb, 52 N·m) per the FSM text. Confirm the bracket moves smoothly.

PHASE 3B SET THE TENSIONER — THE STEP MOST PEOPLE GET WRONG
The Gen 2 3S-GTE uses a hydraulic tensioner with a pinned push rod and a measured setting clearance. This is not a spring
tensioner and it is not "loosen the idler and let it snap over."
3B.1 — Compress and pin the tensioner
In a press, slowly press the push rod in using 100–1,000 kg (220–2,205 lb, 981–9,807 N) of pressure. Align the holes of the
push rod and housing and pass a 1.27 mm hex wrench through to hold the setting position. Release the press.
3B.2 — Preload the No.1 idler and fit the tensioner
Turn the No.1 idler pulley bolt counterclockwise to obtain 180 kg-cm (13 ft-lb, 18 N·m), as far left as the pulley will go, and
temporarily install the tensioner with its two bolts.
CAUTION Apply the torque wrench along the axis running through the No.1 idler pulley bolt and the exhaust
camshaft timing pulley bolt. Off-axis application gives a false reading and the belt will end up mis-tensioned.
3B.3 — First 5/6 turn
Slowly turn the crankshaft pulley 5/6 of a revolution clockwise and align its groove with the ATDC 60° mark of the No.1
timing belt cover.
3B.4 — Set the gap with a 1.90 mm feeler
Insert a 1.90 mm (0.075 in) feeler gauge between the tensioner body and the No.1 idler pulley stopper. Turn the No.1 idler bolt
counterclockwise again to 180 kg-cm (13 ft-lb, 18 N·m). While pushing the tensioner, alternately tighten the two tensioner
bolts to 210 kg-cm (15 ft-lb, 21 N·m). Remove the 1.27 mm hex wrench from the tensioner.

PHASE 4 DEGREE THE INTAKE CAM
The belt is now on factory marks and correctly tensioned, and you have a verified true-TDC zero on the degree wheel.
Everything from here is measurement and small correction.
The arithmetic, once
Concept Rule
Crank vs cam 1 cam degree = 2 crank degrees. The degree wheel reads crank degrees. A 6° wheel correction is a 3°
move at the gear.
Centerline from
open/close Centerline ATDC = (total duration ÷ 2) − opening angle BTDC
Centerline by averaging Take the wheel reading at equal lift each side of peak; the centerline is the average of the two.
Checking lift 1.00 mm (0.039 in) — matches the HKS card so you compare directly with no conversion.
Degree wheel type A 360° continuous wheel reads directly. A 0-180-0 wheel requires you to track which side of TDC you
are on.
4.1 — Set up the indicator
Mount the dial indicator on the No.1 intake shim / bucket, rod as close to vertical as the head allows. A tilted rod under-reads
lift and shifts your measured points.
4.2 — Find and zero peak lift
Rotate clockwise through the intake lobe. Watch for the indicator to stop rising and reverse. Zero the gauge at maximum lift.
Take up all backlash by only ever approaching in the direction of rotation.
4.3 — Method A: peak-lift averaging (primary)
Rotate backward past the lobe, then come forward and record the wheel reading at 1.00 mm before peak. Continue past peak
and record the reading at 1.00 mm after peak. The intake centerline is the average of those two readings, expressed in crank
degrees ATDC.
4.4 — Method B: HKS event check (cross-check)
With the indicator zeroed at zero lift, record the wheel angle where the intake valve reaches 1.00 mm opening and where it
drops back to 1.00 mm closing. Compare directly to the card: 4° BTDC open, 43° ABDC close, 227° total.
CAUTION Method A and Method B must agree within about 1°. If they disagree by more than that, your true-TDC
zero is wrong or the indicator rod is not tracking the shim squarely. Go back to Phase 2 and re-run the piston
stop before touching a cam gear.
4.5 — Compare and correct
Measured intake
centerline Meaning Effect on a turbo 3S
110° ATDC HKS card value Baseline. Correct for the profile as designed.
Less than 110° Intake is advanced More low-end torque, quicker spool, earlier boost onset. Costs top end.
More than 110° Intake is retarded More top-end power. Softer bottom end and later spool.
To correct: loosen the ATS set screws, rotate the hub, re-torque, and re-measure. Never assume the gear moved exactly as
much as you turned it. The ATS gears give ±10 cam degrees, which is ±20 crank degrees — far more range than you need. If
a correction is large enough to consume that range, you have a belt tooth wrong; go back and re-seat the belt.
NOTE For a spool-focused street turbo build, start at the card value of 110° ATDC or up to 4° advanced (106°
ATDC). Sweep in 2° steps on a dyno from there. Do not guess at the wheel.

PHASE 5 DEGREE THE EXHAUST CAM
5.1 — Move the indicator
Relocate to the No.1 exhaust shim / bucket, same rod discipline. Re-zero at peak lift.
5.2 — Measure
Method A: average the wheel readings at 1.00 mm each side of peak → exhaust centerline in crank degrees BTDC.
Method B: record 1.00 mm opening and closing points and compare to the card: 36° BBDC open, 10° ATDC close, 226°
total.
5.3 — Compare and correct
Measured exhaust
centerline Meaning Effect on a turbo 3S
103° BTDC HKS card value Baseline.
More than 103° Exhaust is advanced Blows down earlier. More overlap. Usually hurts a turbo motor.
Less than 103° Exhaust is retarded Widens effective LSA, reduces overlap, typically adds top-end power on a

CAUTION Community starting points conflict — treat them as dyno starting points only. ATS Racing has publicly
stated the exhaust generally likes about 6° retard from true zero, while other sources report 4° advance intake
/ 8° retard exhaust for HKS 264s. These are not equivalent to your card. Degree to the card first, log a
baseline, then sweep exhaust retard in 2° steps on a dyno.
5.4 — Lock and re-verify
Re-torque the ATS set screws, then re-measure both cams with the belt at full operating tension. Gear movement, belt
seating and tensioner settling all shift the numbers. Record the final measured centerlines on the log sheet at the back of this
document.
PHASE 6 VALVE-TO-PISTON CLEARANCE — MANDATORY
DANGER Do not turn this engine on the starter until clearance is verified. A stock Gen 2 3S-GTE is
non-interference. Yours is not. The 5S-FE bottom end combined with HKS 264 lift removes the factory
margin, and at least one documented 5S block + 3S head + HKS 264 build measured hard valve-to-piston
contact at TDC with the belt off.
6.1 — Clay method
With the final valve springs installed, lay modelling clay across the No.1 piston valve pockets, oil the valve faces, fit the head
on a used gasket, and bar the engine through two full revolutions by hand. Section the clay and measure the thinnest
remaining material. Correct for gasket crush.
6.2 — Dial indicator method (more accurate)
Substitute light checking springs, put the indicator on the retainer, and push each valve down to contact at 5° increments
through the overlap region. Record the minimum on both intake and exhaust.
6.3 — If clearance is marginal
Options in order of preference: reduce overlap by retarding the exhaust cam further, fit a thicker head gasket, or machine valve
reliefs. Re-check clearance after any change to cam timing — clearance is a function of the centerlines you just set.

PHASE 7 ATI SUPER DAMPER & REASSEMBLY
7.1 — ATI Super Damper P/N 918529
Deburr and lightly anti-seize the crank snout. The steel hub is an interference fit, not a slip fit — if it slides on by hand, stop
and verify the part number and bore (1.2195 in). Align the indent dimple on the hub with the arrow on the shell decal; the
shell is indexed to the hub through an offset hole and will be out of balance if assembled wrong.
Draw the shell on with the countersunk Torx Plus T-40 screws using Loctite 242. Retain the whole assembly with the OEM
crank bolt at 1,100 kg-cm (80 ft-lb, 108 N·m). Do not substitute a long mandrel bolt — it stretches under heat.
NOTE The elastomer ring may show slightly out of balance until the engine first exceeds about 2,000 rpm, at which
point the inertia weight self-centres. This is normal and documented by ATI.

engine mounting bracket at 530 kg-cm (38 ft-lb, 52 N·m) → RH mounting insulator, nut 530 kg-cm (38 ft-lb, 52 N·m), through

7.3 — First start
