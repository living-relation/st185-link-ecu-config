Your review, point by point

  1. Too tall - bottoms left open. No tray floor and no side slot any more. Each box is now a base (the deck, standing on four corner legs) and a cover. Mount face to cover top: POWER 77.8 mm (was 85), LOGIC 68.9 mm (was 85). Under the POWER deck is 28 mm of open space and under the LOGIC deck 18 mm: that is the room the wires need to bend at your radius (point 4) - the 8 AWG relay feeds set the POWER number. The printed cover itself is only about 42 mm tall.
  2. Rounded, modern. Covers have R13 corners and R5 rounded top edges all round (no sharp edge anywhere on top), bases R10 corners, round legs; the powder-bed freedom is used for the printed cable saddles and the outboard comb under the POWER deck, which also set the bend radius.
  3. Snap-on covers. Four cantilever tongues per cover (slotted into the skirt), each with a half-round catch that drops into a pocket in the base. Off: push a small screwdriver into the slot beside a tongue. The battery block snaps the same way (a screw-down was allowed there; not needed).
  4. Bend radius on every cable. Every heavy cable now leaves its lug dead straight along the lug axis - the AMI output lugs moved under the deck onto brass standoffs, so no cable is bent off a lug inside a box. The only in-box bends are the six relay feeds (AMI to relay terminal 30): one 90 deg bend each, wrapped round a printed R16 saddle. Table below.
  5. Empty renders. Added: both glove-box boxes and the battery block with no relays or fuses, iso and top, plus an underside view.
  6. Etched labels. Every relay, fuse and spare position has its name etched 0.6 mm into the cover top, right above it (24 labels on POWER, 38 on LOGIC, 7 on the battery block). List under "Labels".
  7. Battery block. Style: the Blue Sea Systems 7720 Safety Fuse Block (AMI / MIDI) - my pick (Z9), reasons under "Battery relay block". Open design: a low base with rims on the long sides only and both ends open; no cable holes - cables come in under open notches in the cover ends. Two tabs, one on each long side at mid-length; every cable leaves an end, so nothing crosses a tab. Re-laid on one straight line so its jumper has no bend at all (point 4).
  8. Pieces and wiring. Each box is 2 printed pieces (base, cover); the battery block too. You wire from below: the base is open underneath, so every terminal cavity, standoff and lug is reachable from the wire side. Crimp, push the terminal up into its cavity until it clicks, bolt the lugs under the deck, then mount the base, then plug the relays and fuses in from the top and snap the cover on. Full order under "How you build it".

Bend radius check (every cable at every lug, stud and terminal)

Rule: FAA AC 43.13-1B CHG 1, par. 11-96 aa (p. 11-44/45) - a bundle no tighter than 10 x the OD of its largest wire; where a wire breaks out at a terminal and is suitably supported, 3 x OD. No cable maker publishes a bend radius for M22759/16 Tefzel or for SGX battery cable (checked: Thermax / ProWire M22759/16 sheet, Southwire SPEC 50050, AWC, Prestolite), so this installation rule is used. OD = maximum from the Thermax M22759/16 sheet; 1/0 SGX 13.6 mm. Gauges from the slot map (ENGINE-ROOM-POWER-REDISTRIBUTION 8.1). "Inside R" is from the model; on the two "bend under the deck" rows it is the most room there is.

Empty boxes (terminal slots)

How you build it

POWER box (2 printed pieces)

  1. Press a brass limiter into each of the 4 ears.
  2. Bench, base right way up: lay the bar in its pocket and the 14 pads in theirs. From below, push the M5 x 16 bolts (and the M8 x 20 for the 1/0) up through the hex head pockets and the bar holes.
  3. From below, slide a brass standoff up each hex hole until it meets its pad; from the top, screw an M5 set screw through the pad into it.
  4. Turn the base over (wire side up). Crimp the relay terminals, push each one up into its cavity until it clicks. Crimp the AMI output cables and bolt each lug to the bottom of its standoff (M5 x 10; the hex hole holds the standoff). The six relay feeds go lug -> straight -> round their saddle -> into terminal 30; tie each to its saddle.
  5. Right way up: AMI fuses on the studs, Keps nuts 4 Nm max; 1/0 lug on the M8.
  6. Mount the base in the glove box by its 4 ears (M5 through the limiters); the cables leave underneath between the legs.
  7. Plug the relays and the ACPS fuse in from the top, snap the cover on.

LOGIC box (2 printed pieces)

  1. Limiters in the ears. From below, slide the two strips up into their pockets.
  2. Standoff under each strip end; M5 bolt down from the top (head in its hex pocket) into the standoff; feed lug under the standoff (M5 x 10).
  3. Wire side up: crimp and push in the mini-fuse and micro-relay terminals from below.
  4. Mount by the 4 ears (the two rear legs sit at x 45 / 70 so the Bus 1 / Bus 2 feeds leave the rear straight).
  5. Fuses and micro relays in from the top, snap the cover on.

Battery block (2 printed pieces)

  1. From below, an M6 nut into each of the 2 hex pockets under the relay platform; limiters in the tabs and the 2 flange bosses.
  2. Drop the 3 bolt heads into the pedestal pockets (top), lay the bar and the pad on.
  3. HCR150 on its platform, 2 M6 bolts down through the flange into the captured nuts.
  4. AMI 40 on, jumper (two lugs, one straight 18 mm cable) from the AMI out to terminal 3, pump cable on terminal 5, coil plug on.
  5. Mount by the 2 tabs (passenger side of the trunk, between the rear wall and the battery - Z4), snap the cover on.

To service a relay terminal later: unbolt the base's ears and lift it - the bottom is open, nothing else holds it.

Designed for powder-bed printing

  • Walls: 3 mm base skirt, 6 mm deck plate, 2.5 mm towers, ribs, gussets and cover walls. Nothing under 2.0 mm.
  • No trapped powder: bases are open underneath, covers are open shells; no closed cavity anywhere.
  • Fits: 0.45 mm per side printed to printed (cover over base); 0.4 mm per side metal in a printed pocket.
  • Snaps: tongue 12 wide, 11 long between two 1.2 slots, half-round catch 2.4, about 0.75 mm of engagement - within what PA12 takes repeatedly.
  • Threads: none printed, and no heat-set inserts now (the covers snap). Every bolt goes into metal: standoffs, nuts, captured heads.
  • Torque into metal: pad, standoff and lug are clamped metal to metal; the hex holes only stop the standoffs and bolt heads turning. The HCR150 flange clamps onto brass limiters over captured nuts.
  • Heat: PA12 HDT 95 °C at 1.82 MPa. The 1" x 1/4" copper bar is three times the 1/0 copper; bench check at full load with a thermocouple anyway.
  • Live-part spacing (measured in Fusion): AMI pads 3.0 mm apart (printed rib between), bar to any output 17.1, 1/0 lug to nearest output 8.2, output lugs under the deck 5.5, standoffs 10.0; Bus 1 / Bus 2 strips 4.5, their feed lugs 5.1; battery bar to pad 17.0, HCR150 lugs 5.0.

Interference check (all three units, every printed, copper and bought part): none.
