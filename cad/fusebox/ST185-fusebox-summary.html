How it is split

  • POWER box (outboard, nearest the 1/0 from the RH sill): the bar with all 12 AMI fuses (inboard row feeds the relays, outboard row are the direct outputs), the six Mini ISO relays (k_efi, k_str, k_fan, k_fan2, power - TE F7; heater k_htr - TE Power Relay B changeover, its cavity has the 5th terminal, 87a), the ACPS mini fuse beside the heater relay, one spare relay position. The HCR150 EPS relay stays beside it.
  • LOGIC box (inboard, toward the ECU): Bus 1 and Bus 2 mini-fuse columns on two metal strips, ten micro relay positions (three spare). Each strip has its own feed lug, screwed to the strip underside at its rear end: Bus 1 from the POWER box AMI BUS1 50 A output (8 AWG), Bus 2 from k_efi 87 (10 AWG). These two wires are the only box-to-box links.
  • Cut line: the gap between the micro relays and the first Mini ISO column - every zone keeps its rev 2 position, so the layout, pitches and clearances are unchanged.
  • Battery relay block (trunk, near the battery): the fuel pump relay k_fp = the spare on-hand TE HCR150 (130 A at 85 C against a 19-25 A pump), its AMI 40 fuse on a short bar, M6 battery feed stud. Feed from the kill switch LOAD side, so the trunk switch also stops the pump. Coil- from Link Aux 3 (A19). The pump feed no longer runs from the glove box to the trunk.
  • Wiring: in both glove-box boxes every wire drops through the deck into the 22 mm cable bay and leaves through the forward (firewall) wall.

Designed for powder-bed printing (same rules as rev 2)

  • Walls: 3 mm tray walls and floor (4 mm battery block floor), 2.5 mm ribs and gussets, 8 mm deck plate, 2.5 mm lids plus ribs. Nothing under 2.0 mm.
  • No solid blocks, no trapped powder: ribbed decks open underneath, open-top trays, ribbed lids; no closed cavity.
  • Fits: 0.45 mm per side printed to printed; 0.4 mm per side metal in a printed pocket.
  • Threads: none printed. M3 brass heat-set inserts for lids and deck posts; power studs thread into the metal.
  • Torque goes into metal: every copper joint is a plain hole with a hex bolt from below - head captured in a printed hex pocket (anti-rotation only, on a boss under the deck plate), nut on top clamping fuse ear / lug / copper onto the head. No tapped copper, no hold-down screws. The HCR150 bolts clamp its flange through brass limiters to the trunk floor.
  • Heat: PA12 HDT 95 °C at 1.82 MPa. The 1" x 1/4" copper bar (161 mm²) is three times the 1/0 feed (53 mm²), so it runs far cooler than the cable. Bench check at full load with a thermocouple anyway.
  • Live-part spacing (measured in Fusion): AMI outputs 3.0 mm apart with a printed rib between, bar to any output 17.1 mm, 1/0 lug to nearest output 8.2 mm, output lugs 6.0 mm; Bus 1 to Bus 2 strip 4.5 mm; battery block bar to pad 17.5 mm, HCR150 lugs 5.0 mm (set by the relay's 17 mm terminal pitch).

Interference check (all three units): none between parts.

Heater relay vs fuse: the AMI HTR fuse is 40 A and Power Relay B carries 35 A continuous at 85 °C on its NO contact (ISO 8820-3 overload 1.35 x 35 A for 30 min, so a 40 A fuse still protects it). The blower draws well under that; if the 40 A fuse is only there for wire size, a 30 A AMI would match the relay exactly - your call.

Copper (Y2) - tinned, off the shelf, cut and drilled only

Dan, 2026-10-14 (R76): tinned copper; buy ready stock, no machined bars - cutting, drilling and tapping are OK. So every copper part is a plain rectangle with round holes: no milled lug insets (the lugs now sit on top of the fuse ear), no tapped holes (hex bolts come up from below), no hold-down screws. The model is built around the picked stock. Copper in the model: 403 g.
