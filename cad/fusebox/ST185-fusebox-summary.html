Your review, point by point

  1. Vent slots on the lids. Rows of 2.6 x 14 mm slots through every glove-box lid, placed clear of the etched names (over the gaps between relay columns and along the outboard edge). 2.6 mm keeps fingers and tools out. The battery block in the trunk gets its slots in the long side walls instead of the top, so a spill on the lid does not run in.
  2. Where is the lid for the little one? It was there in rev 4, but only in the one closed picture - the one you looked at was the open view. Rev 5 shows it closed from both sides, from underneath, and lifted off in the exploded view (Battery relay block, below).
  3. Little one: mounts on the short ends. Done - one tab on each short end (M5, brass limiter), set at the rear half of the end so no cable leaves over a tab (the cables leave the ends at the front half). The tabs on the long sides are gone.
  4. How the bus bars, terminals and fuses go in. New: an exploded view of each box with numbered parts, and four step pictures for the POWER box (copper in; turn over, terminals and lugs from below; fuses and relays from the top; pan and cover on). See "How it goes together".
  5. The rest of the cable routing. Every wire is now drawn from its terminal to the box edge. Each harness wire drops straight out of its cavity, bends once (3 x OD, tied into its bundle) and runs in a bundle to one exit: the LOGIC front edge (4 bundles) and the POWER inboard end (13 small bundles). To make that work in the POWER box the relays are turned 90 deg: now no harness wire ever has to cross a relay feed. The bar moved 8.5 mm outboard for it. Heavy cables: OUT row and the 1/0 out the outboard end, Bus feeds out the LOGIC rear. Table and pictures under "Cable routing".
  6. More rugged. Bumper band round the bottom of every lid, vertical ribs up the walls, angular legs (point 9), chamfered tabs, the battery block's two risers joined into one ribbed fuse pedestal.
  7. Why were the studs underneath so long? They are brass standoffs that carry each AMI fuse output down to a cable lug under the deck, so the cable can leave its lug dead straight (your bend-radius rule). In rev 4 every lug sat at the bottom, so every standoff was 40 mm (25 in LOGIC). Rev 5 makes each one only as long as its cable needs:
    • OUT row (7) and the 2 LOGIC feeds: 12 mm. Those cables run straight out of the box, so the lug now sits right under the deck (lug at 37.5 mm, just under the deck plate at 46.5).
    • IN row (7, the relay feeds): still 40 mm - that is what the stack needs. Each feed has to turn up into its relay's terminal 30 from below. Bottom up: lug at 9.5 mm (just above the new belly pan), the R16 bend round its saddle (8 AWG needs 3 x 5.13 = 15.4) up to the deck underside at 32, then the terminal cavity, 19.5 mm, to the pad at 51.5. 51.5 - 9.5 - 2 mm of lug = 40 mm. Shorter only if the 8 AWG feeds bend tighter than your rule.
  8. What are the other 2 parts on the smallest box? The two grey blocks are printed risers (pedestals), part of the base. The first carries the copper bar with the battery stud (M6, battery feed in) and the fuse's input stud; the second carries the copper pad with the fuse's output stud. They lift the AMI fuse so its output lug sits level with the relay's terminal 3, so the jumper is one straight piece of cable. In rev 5 they are one ribbed block (the fuse pedestal) and they are numbered in the exploded view: 5 bar, 6 pad, 7 pedestal.
  9. No more feet - angular legs. Each leg is now a hollow tapered buttress on a skirt face (24 wide at the foot, 16 at the top, outer face sloping out to the foot), with a flat foot and the M5 hole outside it. POWER: on the front and rear faces; LOGIC: on its two long sides, so the front edge and rear stay clear for cables. Open underneath (no trapped powder).
  10. Snap-on covers for everything live underneath. New belly pan under each glove-box box: a 2.5 mm PA12 tray whose wall slides 4 mm up inside the skirt and catches with 4 tongues. It covers every lug, standoff, bolt head and terminal crimp; nearest live part to the pan: 1.5 mm. Cables leave through notches in its wall. To make room under the IN-row bolt heads the boxes grew 4.0 (POWER) and 2.5 mm (LOGIC) in height. The battery block already had a closed floor (nothing live underneath).
  11. Lid labels: unchanged - the rev 4 etched names (your correction: they were fine; the numbers / legend comment was about a route drawing).

How it goes together (point 4)

POWER box (3 printed pieces)

  1. Press a brass limiter into each of the 4 feet.
  2. Base right way up: lay the bar and the 14 pads in their pockets. From below, push the M5 x 16 bolts (and the M8 x 20 for the 1/0) up through the hex head pockets and the bar holes. Slide a standoff up each hex hole (40 mm in the IN row, 12 mm in the OUT row) and screw a set screw down through each pad into it.
  3. Turn the base over. Crimp the relay terminals, push each one up into its cavity until it clicks, and tie the wires into their bundles (they leave the inboard end). Bolt the AMI output lugs under their standoffs (M5 x 10 IN row, M5 x 8 OUT row); each relay feed goes lug -> straight -> round its saddle -> terminal 30.
  4. Right way up: AMI fuses on the studs, Keps nuts 4 Nm max; 1/0 lug on the M8.
  5. Mount the base by its 4 feet, snap the belly pan up into the skirt, plug the relays and the ACPS fuse in, snap the cover on.

LOGIC box (3 printed pieces)

  1. Limiters in the feet. From below, slide the two strips up into their pockets.
  2. 12 mm standoff under each strip end; M5 x 8 strip bolt down from the top (head in its pocket) into the standoff; feed lug under it (M5 x 8).
  3. Wire side up: crimp and push in the mini-fuse and micro-relay terminals; tie each column's wires into its bundle (front edge).
  4. Mount by the 4 feet (long sides), snap the pan on, fuses and micro relays in, snap the cover on.

Battery block (2 printed pieces)

  1. From below, an M6 nut into each of the 2 hex pockets under the relay platform; limiters in the end tabs and the 2 flange bosses.
  2. Drop the 3 bolt heads into the fuse pedestal pockets (top), lay the bar and the pad on.
  3. HCR150 on its platform, 2 M6 bolts down through the flange into the captured nuts.
  4. AMI 40 on, jumper (two lugs, one straight 18 mm cable) from the AMI out to terminal 3, pump cable on terminal 5, coil plug on.
  5. Mount by the 2 end tabs (passenger side of the trunk, between the rear wall and the battery - Z4), snap the cover on.

To service a terminal later: unsnap the pan - every cavity is right there.

Bend radius check (every cable at every lug, stud and terminal)

Rule: FAA AC 43.13-1B CHG 1, par. 11-96 aa (p. 11-44/45) - a bundle no tighter than 10 x the OD of its largest wire; where a wire breaks out at a terminal and is suitably supported, 3 x OD. No cable maker publishes a bend radius for M22759/16 Tefzel or for SGX battery cable (checked: Thermax / ProWire M22759/16 sheet, Southwire SPEC 50050, AWC, Prestolite), so this installation rule is used. OD = maximum from the Thermax M22759/16 sheet; 1/0 SGX 13.6 mm.

Battery relay block

Style (Z9: your pick, S27) - Blue Sea Systems 7720 Safety Fuse Block (AMI / MIDI)

Chosen by Claude (Dan, Z9 2026-10-14: "your pick"; DECISIONS S27). It fits all four asks: open frame (a low base and a cover - no closed tub); lid on, no screws (the cover is a separate part that comes off by hand); no cable holes (its cover breakouts give wire access from three directions, so cables come in under the cover, not through the base); and it takes the same AMI fuse we use, built for wet, vibrating places (marine). 125 x 38 mm for one fuse (Blue Bottle Marine listing BS-7720). The one change: its 2 fixings become 2 end tabs (rev 5: Dan wants the mounts on the short ends), set beside the cables so none leaves over a tab. Not chosen: Bussmann RTMR (a Mini ISO / mini-fuse module - no room for the HCR150 and an AMI); MTA / Littelfuse sealed power-distribution boxes (closed tubs with cable seals, i.e. holes, the opposite of open); Blue Sea MRBF terminal blocks (they sit on the battery post).

Designed for powder-bed printing

  • Walls: 3 mm base skirt, 6 mm deck plate, 2.5 mm towers, ribs, cover and pan walls, 3 mm leg walls. Nothing under 2.0 mm.
  • No trapped powder: bases, legs and pans are open, covers are open shells; no closed cavity anywhere.
  • Fits: 0.45 mm per side printed to printed (cover over base, pan inside the skirt); 0.4 mm per side metal in a printed pocket.
  • Snaps: tongue 12 wide, 11 long between two 1.2 slots, half-round catch 2.4 - covers and pans alike.
  • Threads: none printed, no heat-set inserts. Every bolt goes into metal: standoffs, nuts, captured heads.
  • Heat: PA12 HDT 95 °C at 1.82 MPa. The 1" x 1/4" copper bar is three times the 1/0 copper; bench check at full load with a thermocouple anyway. The vent slots let the relay coil heat out.
