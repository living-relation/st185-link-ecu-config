How it is built

  • Tray + lid: machined 6061, same style as the VRC / CSB3 enclosures. A 22 mm cable bay under the deck; every wire leaves through one grommet strip in the forward (firewall) wall. The 1/0 feed enters through its own grommet in the outboard end wall.
  • Isolation: no conductor touches the aluminium. The printed PA6-GF deck carries every terminal, the bar and the pads; the box itself is isolated from the body by nylon shoulder washers on the 4 ears.
  • Power zone (outboard): one 25 x 6 aluminium bar along the depth, M8 1/0 input at its forward end, AMI fuses on both sides. Inboard row feeds the F7 relays (short internal leads); outboard row is the direct outputs. Each output stud sits on an aluminium pad with a ring-lug inset machined in (bus bar design B style): the lug drops into the pocket, the fuse ear clamps it, the barrel exits straight into a wire hole through the deck.
  • F7 relays (middle): 2 x 4 grid; the 8th spot is the ACPS mini fuse, beside the heater relay (L3B).
  • Logic zone (inboard): Bus 1 and Bus 2 mini-fuse columns (8.1 mm pitch) on 8 x 4 aluminium strips inside the deck, then 10 micro relay positions.

Open choices for Dan

  1. One box or two? The task card says "the 2 custom boxes"; plan rev 2 (your 10-13 answer, fewer boxes) is ONE box. Modeled as one. Splitting it at the F7 column gives a 182 mm power box and a 78 mm logic box, same depth.
  2. Power bar metal: aluminium 6061 here (matches the bus bar). Plan rev 2 said copper C110 25 x 3. Copper carries more current for its size; aluminium is lighter and the same metal as the bus bar. 25 x 6 Al (150 mm2) carries more than the 1/0 copper (53 mm2) that feeds it.
