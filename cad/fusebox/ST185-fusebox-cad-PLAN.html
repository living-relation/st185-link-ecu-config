One custom fuse + relay box: plan and to-scale mock-up

One 3D-printed box replaces the Eaton RFRM, 3 MTA modules and both LMI stacks. It has a power zone (all fuses of 30 A and up, as AMI stud fuses on one copper bar, plus the six F7 relays) and a logic zone (mini fuses on two bus strips plus the micro relays). One M8 input, no box-to-box jumpers, one lid. The HCR150 EPS relay stays beside it. The fuel pump relay moves out to an SSR at the battery box.

What changed from rev 1 (Dan, 2026-10-13)

  • Custom, not off-the-shelf (L5): R18 Option B is replaced. This needs a new DECISIONS rule and a CabinPower redraw, done in the repo when the CAD is approved.
  • One box, not two: fewer boxes is the goal. The four jumpers between the boxes (L1 to Bus 1, k_efi 87 to Bus 2, Bus 0 tap, heater 87) become short links inside the box.
  • Fuel pump out (L3A): k_fp and its 20 A fuse leave the box for an SSR at the battery box. ETB 10 A moves onto Bus 1. Bus 0 and its unfused tap are gone, which also settles L2.
  • A/C pressure-switch fuse at its source (L3B): the ACPS fuse sits at the end of the logic zone next to the heater relay. The old 10 AWG jumper becomes a ~50 mm 12 AWG link.
  • Orientation from the approved routes (L4): the power zone is at the outboard end, the logic zone inboard, and wires exit along the forward (firewall) edge.

3. Orientation, from the approved routes

  • Outboard end = power zone + M8 input: the 1/0 comes up the RH sill to the bus bar in the glove box (Route 1), and the heavy outputs leave nearby. Blower at the right kick (old R/B4 spot); fans, EPS, headlights and horns through harness C's RH fender grommet (Route 2); HCR150 for EPS on the same side.
  • Inboard end = logic zone: the relay coil wires and the ECU-rail fuses (SW12, INJ, COP, CSB, DEV) run to the ECU in the centre dash (Routes 5 / 6). LAM goes to bulkhead B on the RH firewall.
  • Forward edge = wire exit: every run leaves forward or down. The firewall items (bulkhead B, RADLOK, RH fender grommet) are ahead. The left-kick run (AM1, AM2, J/B1 HOT, RTR, dome, hazard, W-G, W-L, reverse) goes across under the dash, as harness C's cabin part already does (Route 2).
  • The exact PDB / bus-bar spot in the glove box is still open in Route 1. The box only needs the bus bar at its outboard end.

Recommendation: AMI. Parts cost is about the same (about $80 vs about $60-95). AMI needs 12 crimps instead of 36 and no special crimper. The fuses bolt to the bar, so there is no blade contact to loosen. The MAXI terminals found so far are MTA parts from UK / AU sellers; none were found from TE or Dan's approved suppliers. MAXI saves about 50 mm of depth and has cheaper replacement fuses. "Estimate" rows are not priced from a source.

5. Build approach (unchanged from rev 1)

  • Wire side: loose crimped terminals, rear-fed through the floor, held by printed front stops plus a sliding TPA comb. No edge connectors.
  • Bussing: power zone = one 25 x 3 mm C110 copper bar with M5 studs and the M8 input. Logic zone = two 8 x 4 mm bars under the fuse rows, one short lead + M3/M4 ring per bussed fuse. No PCB, no solder on power paths.
  • Terminals: TE 280756-4 / 280755-4 (F7 30/87), 42281-1 (F7 85/86, micro 30/87), 4.8 mm and 2.8 mm receptacles TBD, as in rev 1's parts table.
  • Material: ASA; PA6-GF for the power zone if the bar runs warm; not PA-CF (static-dissipative) [S13].
  • Mounting: 4 M5 ears; molded labels on the deck, legend on the lid.

6. Footprint

One box: 370 x 148 = 54,760 mm², about 3 % less than today's kit (about 56,600 mm² plus MTA brackets). It also has 1 lid instead of 6 modules, 4 mounting screws, and 1 feed instead of 5.

7. Open questions for Dan

  1. L1 - AMI or MAXI? Cost is now above; AMI is recommended.
  2. L6 - Fuel pump circuit at the battery box (sized for running current AND inrush):
    • Running current: 19.0 A rated at 40 psi / 13.5 V; up to 25 A quoted by vendors for this pump. It rises with pressure (up to 73.5 psi at 30 psi boost) and voltage (14+ V charging). Design figure: 25 A continuous.
    • Inrush: no published figure for this pump (none found from Walbro, TI or vendors). A brushed motor starts at several times its running current for tens of ms. Measure it on the bench (clamp meter with inrush / peak capture, or shunt + scope) before the SSR is bought.
    • Fuse: MAXI 40 A (Bussmann MAX, 20-80 A range [S8]), not a 30 A mini. The mini range stops at 30 A (ATM 2-30 A [S7]), which would run at about 83 % of rating with 25 A in a warm trunk. ISO 8820-3 blade-fuse opening limits (Eaton ATM): 135 % opens in 0.75-600 s, 200 % in 0.15-5 s, 350 % in 0.04-0.5 s, 600 % in 0.02-0.1 s. With 40 A, 25 A running is 63 %, and a 150 A start spike is under 400 %, well clear of the 600 % / 20 ms minimum.
    • Wire: 10 AWG, protected by 40 A per the repo sizing table (10 AWG = 40 A max).
    • SSR spec: DC output, at least 40 A continuous AFTER its heat derating (needs a heatsink or a metal mount), a non-repetitive surge rating (usually given for 10 ms) above the measured inrush, low on-resistance (heat = I²R at 25 A), off-state leakage too small to turn the pump, and an input that works with the Link's low-side drive on A19.
    • Turn-off spike: switching off a motor makes an inductive voltage spike that can kill a DC SSR. Put a freewheel diode (rated for the pump current) across the pump feed at the battery box, unless the SSR's datasheet says it is built in. This is the load side; R25's "no flyback diodes" is about relay coils on the ECU side.
    • Needs: an R15 exception (fuse in the trunk) and the SSR picked with st185-pick-parts once the inrush is measured.
